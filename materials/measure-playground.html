The Measure Playground

Two ideas, made draggable: a σ-algebra is a resolution on the space of outcomes, and expectation is an area under the quantile curve.

Act I · ResolutionA σ-algebra is a pixelation

The blob below is a sample space Ω, and the colouring is a random variable X(ω) — a number attached to every outcome. Drag the slider: at each resolution you see not X itself but its per-pixel average — the best version of X that this resolution can express. A random variable is measurable at a resolution exactly when it is constant on each pixel: nothing of it is lost.

Act II · IntegrationExpectation is the area under the quantile curve

Take Ω = [0,1] with uniform mass and let X = Q(u) be the quantile (inverse-CDF) map of a two-bump mixture. Then E[X] = ∫₀¹ Q(u) du: the signed area under the curve. Lebesgue's construction is the staircase: approximate Q by a simple function (finitely many values), add value × width, refine.

The two acts are one story. Probability is a measure (area on Ω); a random variable is a map out of Ω; expectation is the integral of that map against the measure — computable on the blob (Act I: average the colouring) or, after transport to [0,1], as an area under a curve (Act II). Same number, two homes.
