Sampling as a Map

Generative AI in its simplest form: first generate by ancestral sampling (flip a coin, then draw), then discover the same generator is a single deterministic map applied to uniform randomness.

Act I · HierarchyConditional generation: coin first, Gaussian second

A hierarchical (Bayesian) model, and the simplest conditional generator:

z ∼ Bernoulli(π),  x | z ∼ N(μz, σ²)

Sample the latent coin z, then sample x from the chosen component. The colors below remember each sample's ancestry — and the marginal that emerges is exactly the two-component mixture p(x) = π N(x; μ₁, σ²) + (1−π) N(x; μ₂, σ²).

Act II · The mapThe same generator as one deterministic function

Now remove the coin. Take a single uniform number u ∼ Uniform(0, 1) and push it through the inverse of the cumulative distribution function:

x = F⁻¹(u),   F(x) = π Φ((x−μ₁)/σ) + (1−π) Φ((x−μ₂)/σ)

Below, uniform points enter on the vertical axis, travel to the curve u = F(x), and drop to the x-axis. The histogram they build is the same mixture — no coin anywhere. Now slide σ → 0: the curve becomes a staircase, and the map becomes x = sign(u − π) — W01's two-point dataset, generated by thresholding a uniform.

The definition hiding in this demo. A generative model is a map that transforms simple randomness into data. Here the map is analytic because the distribution is one-dimensional and known. Deep generative models keep the recipe and learn the map: GANs and VAEs map Gaussian noise through a network; normalizing flows make the map invertible; and W01's reverse probability-flow ODE is exactly such a map — from a Gaussian to the data — built from a learned denoiser. That family is where this course is headed.
