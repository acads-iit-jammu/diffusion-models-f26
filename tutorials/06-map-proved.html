The Deterministic Map, Proved

A standalone, self-contained tutorial. It constructs the deterministic map that turns uniform noise into samples from a symmetric two-component Gaussian mixture, proves that as the noise scale vanishes this map degenerates into the sign function, and then shows that the score-driven reverse flow — the sampler at the heart of diffusion models — computes the very same transport, incrementally, in a sense made exact in its second half.

The claim, and the route

Fix \(\sigma > 0\) and consider the symmetric two-component Gaussian mixture with equal weights and means \(\pm 1\):

\[ p_\sigma(x) = \tfrac12\,\mathcal{N}(x; -1, \sigma^2) + \tfrac12\,\mathcal{N}(x; +1, \sigma^2), \qquad F_\sigma(x) = \tfrac12\,\Phi\!\Big(\frac{x+1}{\sigma}\Big) + \tfrac12\,\Phi\!\Big(\frac{x-1}{\sigma}\Big), \]

where \(\Phi\) is the standard normal CDF. (Throughout, \(p_\sigma\) denotes both the density and its law; pushforwards act on the law.) Two statements are to be proved:

  1. There exists a smooth, strictly increasing map \(T_\sigma\) with \(T_\sigma(U) \sim p_\sigma\) for \(U \sim \text{Uniform}(0,1)\).
  2. As \(\sigma \downarrow 0\), \(T_\sigma(u) \to \operatorname{sign}(u - \tfrac12)\) for every fixed \(u \in (0,1) \setminus \{\tfrac12\}\) — the generator of the two-point distribution \(\tfrac12\,\delta_{-1} + \tfrac12\,\delta_{+1}\), which is the \(\sigma \to 0\) limit of the mixture.

The route runs in five steps, each a plain claim backed by formal results: (1) sampling is a map — a uniform pushed through an inverse CDF acquires that CDF (Theorem 1); (2) our mixture admits the map — \(F_\sigma\) is a smooth bijection, so \(T_\sigma\) exists and is smooth (Proposition 2); (3) symmetry halves the work (Lemma 3); (4) a squeeze argument gives the sign-function limit (Theorem 4), with its distributional consequences (Corollary 5); (5) the fine structure — the shape of the stairs and the forming jump. Steps 1–5 build and analyze the one-shot map; the second half of the tutorial then earns the deeper claim — the score-driven reverse flow is the same map, and the practical sampler is its numerical solution. The theorems are the scaffolding; the argument is the building.

Step 1 — Sampling is a map

The entire construction rests on one line: a uniform random number pushed through an inverse CDF is a sample from that CDF. Stated exactly:

Theorem 1 (probability integral transform). Let \(F\) be a continuous, strictly increasing CDF mapping \(\mathbb{R}\) onto \((0,1)\), and let \(U \sim \text{Uniform}(0,1)\). Then \(X = F^{-1}(U)\) has CDF \(F\).

Proof. Monotonicity of \(F\) gives the equivalence \(F^{-1}(u) \le x \iff u \le F(x)\). Hence

\[ \mathbb{P}\big(F^{-1}(U) \le x\big) = \mathbb{P}\big(U \le F(x)\big) = F(x). \qquad\square \]

(For CDFs that are not strictly increasing or not continuous, the same conclusion holds with the generalized inverse \(F^{-1}(u) = \inf\{x : F(x) \ge u\}\); Exercise 1.)

Step 2 — The mixture admits the map

Step 1 applies to our target as soon as \(F_\sigma\) is invertible — and it is, smoothly, at every positive noise level. That is the whole content of the supporting result:

Proposition 2 (existence and smoothness). \(F_\sigma\) is a smooth, strictly increasing bijection of \(\mathbb{R}\) onto \((0,1)\). Consequently \(T_\sigma := F_\sigma^{-1}\) exists, is smooth, and satisfies

\[ T_\sigma'(u) \;=\; \frac{1}{p_\sigma\big(T_\sigma(u)\big)} \;>\; 0 . \]

Proof. \(F_\sigma' = p_\sigma\), which is everywhere positive; hence \(F_\sigma\) is strictly increasing. Both \(\Phi\) terms tend to \(0\) as \(x \to -\infty\) and to \(1\) as \(x \to +\infty\), so \(F_\sigma\) maps onto \((0,1)\) and is a bijection. Smoothness of \(T_\sigma\) and the derivative formula follow from the inverse function theorem. \(\square\)

The derivative formula deserves a comment, because it already contains the tutorial’s central picture. \(T_\sigma\) is steepest exactly where the density is smallest. Between the two bumps of \(p_\sigma\) the density is nearly zero, so \(F_\sigma\) is nearly flat there — a plateau at height \(\tfrac12\) — and its inverse is correspondingly near-vertical. As \(\sigma\) shrinks, the graph of \(T_\sigma\) therefore sharpens into a staircase: two long, nearly flat treads (inputs \(u < \tfrac12\) landing near \(-1\), inputs \(u > \tfrac12\) landing near \(+1\)) joined by an ever-steeper riser at \(u = \tfrac12\). The remaining steps make this picture exact: the treads flatten onto the values \(\pm 1\) (Step 4), and each tread retains a \(\sigma\)-sized microstructure (Step 5).

Step 3 — Symmetry halves the work

The target is symmetric about the origin, and its map inherits the symmetry: the map is odd about the center point \(u = \tfrac12\), so any limit statement proved on one side transfers to the other. The supporting computation:

Lemma 3 (symmetry). \(F_\sigma(-x) = 1 - F_\sigma(x)\) for all \(x\); consequently \(T_\sigma(1-u) = -T_\sigma(u)\) for all \(u \in (0,1)\), and \(T_\sigma(\tfrac12) = 0\).

Proof. Using \(\Phi(-t) = 1 - \Phi(t)\) on each term,

\[ F_\sigma(-x) = \tfrac12\,\Phi\!\Big(\frac{1-x}{\sigma}\Big) + \tfrac12\,\Phi\!\Big(\frac{-1-x}{\sigma}\Big) = \tfrac12\Big[1 - \Phi\!\Big(\frac{x-1}{\sigma}\Big)\Big] + \tfrac12\Big[1 - \Phi\!\Big(\frac{x+1}{\sigma}\Big)\Big] = 1 - F_\sigma(x). \]

Applying \(T_\sigma\) to both sides of \(F_\sigma(-x) = 1 - F_\sigma(x)\) with \(u = F_\sigma(x)\) yields \(T_\sigma(1-u) = -T_\sigma(u)\); setting \(u = \tfrac12\) gives \(T_\sigma(\tfrac12) = -T_\sigma(\tfrac12) = 0\). \(\square\)

By the lemma, it suffices to analyze \(u < \tfrac12\).

Step 4 — In the limit, the map is the sign function

The idea is a squeeze: to show \(T_\sigma(u) \to -1\), trap \(T_\sigma(u)\) between \(-1 - \varepsilon\) and \(-1 + \varepsilon\) by evaluating \(F_\sigma\) at those two points and letting monotonicity do the rest. Two evaluations, one theorem.

Theorem 4 (the limit is the sign function). For every fixed \(u \in (0,1)\) with \(u \ne \tfrac12\),

\[ \lim_{\sigma \downarrow 0} T_\sigma(u) \;=\; \operatorname{sign}\big(u - \tfrac12\big). \]

Proof. By Lemma 3 it suffices to treat \(u < \tfrac12\) and show \(T_\sigma(u) \to -1\). Fix \(\varepsilon \in (0,1)\). Evaluate \(F_\sigma\) at the two points \(-1 \pm \varepsilon\) and let \(\sigma \downarrow 0\):

\[ F_\sigma(-1+\varepsilon) = \tfrac12\,\Phi\!\Big(\frac{\varepsilon}{\sigma}\Big) + \tfrac12\,\Phi\!\Big(\frac{\varepsilon - 2}{\sigma}\Big) \;\longrightarrow\; \tfrac12 \cdot 1 + \tfrac12 \cdot 0 = \tfrac12, \]

since \(\varepsilon > 0\) and \(\varepsilon - 2 < 0\). Because \(u < \tfrac12\), there is a \(\sigma_1 > 0\) with \(F_\sigma(-1+\varepsilon) > u\) for all \(\sigma < \sigma_1\); by strict monotonicity of \(F_\sigma\) this gives \(T_\sigma(u) < -1 + \varepsilon\). Similarly,

\[ F_\sigma(-1-\varepsilon) = \tfrac12\,\Phi\!\Big(\frac{-\varepsilon}{\sigma}\Big) + \tfrac12\,\Phi\!\Big(\frac{-2-\varepsilon}{\sigma}\Big) \;\longrightarrow\; 0, \]

so there is a \(\sigma_2 > 0\) with \(F_\sigma(-1-\varepsilon) < u\) for \(\sigma < \sigma_2\), whence \(T_\sigma(u) > -1 - \varepsilon\). For \(\sigma < \min(\sigma_1, \sigma_2)\) we conclude \(|T_\sigma(u) - (-1)| < \varepsilon\). \(\square\)

At the single point \(u = \tfrac12\), Lemma 3 gives \(T_\sigma(\tfrac12) = 0\) for every \(\sigma\); since \(\mathbb{P}(U = \tfrac12) = 0\), this point is irrelevant to the distributional statement below.

Consequences: the discrete generator as a limit of smooth ones

Corollary 5 (modes of convergence). Let \(S(u) = \operatorname{sign}(u - \tfrac12)\). Theorem 4 states \(T_\sigma(U) \to S(U)\) almost surely. Almost-sure convergence implies convergence in distribution, and \(S(U)\) takes the values \(\pm 1\) with probability \(\tfrac12\) each; hence

\[ p_\sigma \;\Longrightarrow\; \tfrac12\,\delta_{-1} + \tfrac12\,\delta_{+1} \qquad (\sigma \downarrow 0), \]

recovering the two-point distribution \(\tfrac12\,\delta_{-1} + \tfrac12\,\delta_{+1}\) as the weak limit of the mixtures. The discrete generator \(x = S(u)\) — thresholding a uniform — is thereby exhibited as the pointwise limit of smooth generators.

Step 5 — The fine structure: how the stairs and the jump form

Two refinements sharpen the qualitative theorem.

The shape of each stair. Fix \(u < \tfrac12\) and write \(x = T_\sigma(u)\), which by Theorem 4 lies near \(-1\) for small \(\sigma\); in particular \(x \le 0\) eventually, so the far component satisfies \(0 < \tfrac12\Phi\big(\tfrac{x-1}{\sigma}\big) \le \tfrac12\Phi(-1/\sigma)\), exponentially small. From \(u = F_\sigma(x)\),

\[ \Phi\!\Big(\frac{x+1}{\sigma}\Big) = 2u - 2\eta_\sigma, \qquad 0 < \eta_\sigma \le \tfrac12\,\Phi(-1/\sigma), \]

and therefore

\[ T_\sigma(u) \;=\; -1 + \sigma\,\Phi^{-1}\!\big(2u - 2\eta_\sigma\big) \;\approx\; -1 + \sigma\,\Phi^{-1}(2u). \]

Each tread of the limiting staircase carries a copy of the Gaussian quantile function, compressed by the factor \(\sigma\) — asymptotically, the inverse-CDF map coincides with the two-stage sampler that first chooses the component from \(u\) (below or above \(\tfrac12\)) and then applies that component’s own Gaussian quantile.

The forming jump. At the center, Proposition 2 gives

\[ T_\sigma'(\tfrac12) = \frac{1}{p_\sigma(0)} = \sigma\sqrt{2\pi}\; e^{1/(2\sigma^2)} \;\longrightarrow\; \infty , \]

so the slope at \(u = \tfrac12\) blows up as the jump forms. Convergence in Theorem 4 is uniform on compact subsets of \((0,1) \setminus \{\tfrac12\}\) (by uniform continuity of \(\Phi^{-1}\) on compact subintervals together with the uniform exponential bound on the far component) but not across \(u = \tfrac12\): a boundary layer of width shrinking with \(\sigma\) always remains — the same non-uniform sharpening by which the smooth function \(\tanh(x/\sigma^2)\) approaches \(\operatorname{sign}(x)\).

The score-based flow is the same map

The objective. The first half of this tutorial constructed one generator: the quantile map \(T_\sigma\). This section confronts it with a second construction — the score-driven flow at the heart of diffusion models — built from entirely different ingredients, and settles their exact relationship. First, the problem both constructions solve, stated as a problem:

The one-shot transport problem. Given the source law \(p_{\sigma_{\max}}\) and the target law \(p_{\sigma_{\min}}\), find a deterministic map \(M\) such that if the input is distributed as \(p_{\sigma_{\max}}\), the output \(M(X)\) is distributed as \(p_{\sigma_{\min}}\) — in symbols, \(M_\# \, p_{\sigma_{\max}} = p_{\sigma_{\min}}\), where \(M_\# \mu\) denotes the pushforward: the law of \(M(X)\) when \(X \sim \mu\).

Definition (quantile transport). For laws \(\mu, \nu\) on \(\mathbb{R}\) with continuous, strictly increasing CDFs \(F_\mu, F_\nu\), the quantile transport from \(\mu\) to \(\nu\) is

\[ Q_{\mu \to \nu} \;:=\; F_\nu^{-1} \circ F_\mu . \]

It solves the transport problem — \(F_\mu(X) \sim \text{Uniform}(0,1)\) for \(X \sim \mu\) (the probability integral transform read forward), and Theorem 1 then carries the uniform to \(\nu\) — and by Proposition 7 (proved below) it is the unique continuous, strictly increasing solution. It matches each \(\mu\)-quantile to the same \(\nu\)-quantile: median to median, 90th percentile to 90th percentile.

The one-shot solution to our problem is \(Q_{p_{\sigma_{\max}} \to\, p_{\sigma_{\min}}} = F_{\sigma_{\min}}^{-1} \circ F_{\sigma_{\max}}\): one formula, evaluated in a single stroke. The incremental solution flows particles down the noise ladder along the score of the current level. Two functions describe that local geometry. The posterior-mean denoiser

\[ D_\sigma(x) := \mathbb{E}[X_0 \mid X_\sigma = x] = \tanh(x/\sigma^2) \]

is the conditional mean of the clean point given a noisy observation — the quantity diffusion models learn — and the score is its rescaled displacement,

\[ s_\sigma(x) = \partial_x \log p_\sigma(x) = \frac{D_\sigma(x) - x}{\sigma^2} \]

(differentiate \(\log p_\sigma\) to check). One caution about the resulting motion: it is not always “toward the nearer bump”. At high noise (\(\sigma > 1\)) the reverse field contracts everything toward the center; only once \(\sigma\) drops below \(1\) does the origin destabilize and the two clusters separate — the stability computation appears at the end of Layer 1. On the surface the two constructions look like different algorithms that happen to share endpoints. The claim — stated first, then earned in three layers — is much stronger.

Before any claims, two conventions and a cast of characters. Clock: \(\tau = \sigma^2\) is the variance clock; for \(\tau\)-indexed objects we write \(\bar p_\tau := p_{\sqrt{\tau}}\), \(\bar F_\tau := F_{\sqrt{\tau}}\), and \(\bar s_\tau := \partial_x \log \bar p_\tau\), so that subscripts never silently change meaning. Ladder: \(\sigma_{\max} = \sigma_0 > \sigma_1 > \cdots > \sigma_K = \sigma_{\min}\) denotes a descending sequence of noise levels (fixed when Layer 3 needs it; until then \(\Psi_{a \to b}\) makes sense for any two levels \(a > b\)). Five distinct objects appear in this section, and they are easy to blur into one another:

Object Symbol What kind of thing it is
score \(s_\sigma(x) = \partial_x \log p_\sigma(x)\) a function of position, one per noise level — the local steering information; for our mixture, \((\tanh(x/\sigma^2) - x)/\sigma^2\)
velocity field \(v = -\tfrac12\, \bar s_\tau\) (variance clock) an arrow attached to every (position, level) pair — built from the score; the sampler traverses it toward smaller \(\tau\)
the flow (ODE) \(dx/d\tau = v(x, \tau)\) a dynamical rule: from a starting point, integrate to get a trajectory
flow map \(\Psi_{a \to b}\) a function \(\mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}\): feed in a position at level \(a\), integrate the ODE down to level \(b\), read off where the trajectory ends
pushforward \(\Psi_\# \mu\) not a new object but an operation: the law of the outputs when the inputs have law \(\mu\)

So \(\Psi_{\sigma_0 \to \sigma_K}\) is not the score, and not a measure: it is the deterministic input–output function obtained by integrating the score-built velocity field from level \(\sigma_0\) down to \(\sigma_K\); \(\Psi_{\sigma_k \to \sigma_{k+1}}\) is the same construction run over a single leg. The pushforward is how any map — this one included — acts on distributions, and the sentence “the flow solves the transport problem” means exactly \(\Psi_\#\, p_{\sigma_{\max}} = p_{\sigma_{\min}}\): it is a candidate \(M\) for the transport problem stated above.

Key takeaway

They are one and the same solution, at three levels of resolution. (i) Identity: in one dimension the exact probability-flow map is the quantile transport \(F_{\sigma_{\min}}^{-1} \circ F_{\sigma_{\max}}\) — at every positive noise level, not only in a limit. (ii) Mechanism: the flow conserves the quantile: \(u = \bar F_\tau(x_\tau)\) is constant along every trajectory, so the one-shot map jumps each point to its quantile’s final position while the flow tracks the same quantile continuously through every intermediate law. (iii) Consequence: every adjacent pair of noise levels admits its own quantile transport; each step of the practical sampler is the first-order approximation of that per-step quantile map; and the telescoping identity composes the per-step maps back into the one-shot transport — so reverse diffusion is, literally, a numerical solution of the one-shot transport problem.

The layers prove, in order: the flow map equals the quantile transport \(Q\) (Layer 1); the reason is that it conserves quantiles (Layer 2); and it factorizes leg by leg, with the sampler as its Euler discretization (Layer 3).

The descent, in one chain. The whole section — many-step reverse diffusion down to the one-shot inverse CDF — is the following sequence of constructions and identifications, each arrow one step:

\[ \begin{gathered} \text{score } s_\sigma = \partial_x \log p_\sigma \\[-1pt] \big\downarrow \; {\scriptstyle \text{scale}} \\[-1pt] \text{velocity field } v = -\tfrac12\, \bar s_\tau \\[-1pt] \big\downarrow \; {\scriptstyle \text{impose as dynamics}} \\[-1pt] \text{ODE } \; dx/d\tau = v(x, \tau) \\[-1pt] \big\downarrow \; {\scriptstyle \text{integrate — exactly: the flow; per frozen step: Euler, the } K\text{-step sampler}} \\[-1pt] \text{flow map } \Psi_{\sigma_{\max} \to \sigma_{\min}} \\[-1pt] \big\downarrow \; {\scriptstyle \text{pushforward } \Psi_\# p_{\sigma_{\max}} = p_{\sigma_{\min}} \;+\; \text{monotone (no crossing)} \;\Rightarrow\; \text{rigidity}} \\[-1pt] \text{quantile transport } Q = F_{\sigma_{\min}}^{-1} \circ F_{\sigma_{\max}} \quad {\scriptstyle \text{(each leg identified by rigidity; legs composed by telescoping)}} \\[-1pt] \big\downarrow \; {\scriptstyle \sigma_{\min} \downarrow 0} \\[-1pt] \operatorname{sign}(x) \;\; {\scriptstyle \text{(the two-point generator)}} \end{gathered} \]

Read downward, the chain is how reverse diffusion is built; the pushforward-plus-monotonicity arrow is where it is identified with the one-shot map — note that the pushforward property alone would not suffice (many maps carry \(p_{\sigma_{\max}}\) to \(p_{\sigma_{\min}}\)); it is pushforward together with monotonicity that forces \(\Psi = Q\), by Proposition 7. Everything below is this chain, one arrow at a time.

Layer 1 — the identity

(premise) — the flow transports the family. Everything below uses one fact about the flow: it reproduces the prescribed marginals, \((\Psi_{a \to b})_\#\, p_a = p_b\). This is the defining property of the probability-flow field, verified by one rearrangement: with \(v = -\tfrac12\, \bar s_\tau\) we have \(\bar p_\tau v = -\tfrac12\, \partial_x \bar p_\tau\), so the continuity equation \(\partial_\tau \bar p_\tau + \partial_x(\bar p_\tau v) = 0\) is exactly the heat equation \(\partial_\tau \bar p_\tau = \tfrac12\, \partial_x^2 \bar p_\tau\), which the Gaussian-mixture family satisfies (the componentwise check opens Layer 2). An ensemble started with law \(p_a\) and moved by the ODE therefore arrives with law \(p_b\); Layer 2 sharpens this ensemble statement to a particlewise conservation law.

(a) — flows cannot cross, so the flow map is monotone.

Proposition 6 (flows do not cross). For \(\sigma_{\min} > 0\), let \(\Psi\) denote the exact probability-flow ODE map carrying noise level \(\sigma_{\max}\) down to \(\sigma_{\min}\) (the field \(v = -\tfrac12\, \bar s_\tau\), integrated toward smaller \(\tau\), exact score). Then \(\Psi\) is continuous and strictly increasing.

Proof sketch. The field is smooth, and its spatial derivative is globally bounded on the band: \(s_\sigma'(x) = \sigma^{-4}\operatorname{sech}^2(x/\sigma^2) - \sigma^{-2}\) is bounded uniformly for \(\sigma \in [\sigma_{\min}, \sigma_{\max}]\), so solutions exist across the whole leg and are unique. If two trajectories starting at \(x < x'\) met at some time, running the ODE backwards from the meeting point would violate uniqueness; hence trajectories preserve order, and \(\Psi\) is strictly increasing. Continuity of the flow map in the initial condition is standard ODE theory. \(\square\)

(b) — a monotone transport between two given laws is unique.

Proposition 7 (monotone transport is rigid). Let \(T\) be continuous and strictly increasing with \(T_\# \mu = \nu\), where \(\mu, \nu\) have continuous, strictly increasing CDFs \(F_\mu, F_\nu\). Then \(T = F_\nu^{-1} \circ F_\mu\).

Proof. \(F_\nu(T(x)) = \nu\big((-\infty, T(x)]\big) = \mu\big(T^{-1}(-\infty, T(x)]\big) = \mu\big((-\infty, x]\big) = F_\mu(x)\), using monotonicity in the middle step. Apply \(F_\nu^{-1}\). \(\square\)

(c) — identify the flow, then take the limit.

Corollary 8. The end-to-end flow map satisfies \(\Psi_{\sigma_{\max} \to \sigma_{\min}} = F_{\sigma_{\min}}^{-1} \circ F_{\sigma_{\max}}\): the flow map of the probability-flow ODE is the quantile transport, at every positive noise level. Composing with a uniform parametrization of the source — write \(x_T\) for a starting position at the top level, \(x_T = F_{\sigma_{\max}}^{-1}(u)\) — the composite \(u \mapsto \Psi\big(F_{\sigma_{\max}}^{-1}(u)\big)\) is exactly the map \(T_{\sigma_{\min}}\) of Step 2: uniform noise in, mixture sample out, through the flow. Theorem 4 therefore applies verbatim: as \(\sigma_{\min} \downarrow 0\),

\[ \Psi(x_T) \;\longrightarrow\; \operatorname{sign}(x_T) \qquad \text{for every } x_T \ne 0, \]

since \(F_{\sigma_{\max}}(0) = \tfrac12\) by symmetry. The tanh-driven flow converges pointwise to the deterministic two-point generator. Its separatrix \(x_T = 0\) — where the score vanishes by symmetry — is an equilibrium of the reverse dynamics whose character changes with the noise level: since \(\tfrac12 s_\sigma'(0) = \tfrac12(\sigma^{-4} - \sigma^{-2})\), the origin attracts while \(\sigma > 1\), is neutral at \(\sigma = 1\), and repels once \(\sigma < 1\) — the two regimes announced in the objective.

The identity, applied to the example. Corollary 8 is not only a structural fact — for this tutorial’s mixture it is a computable formula. Plugging the CDFs in:

\[ \Psi(x) = F_{\sigma_{\min}}^{-1}\!\Big( \tfrac12\, \Phi\big(\tfrac{x+1}{\sigma_{\max}}\big) + \tfrac12\, \Phi\big(\tfrac{x-1}{\sigma_{\max}}\big) \Big). \]

The outer inverse has no closed form (numerically, one bisects it) — but the stair asymptotics of Step 5, applied here to the transport itself for the first time, give a closed-form asymptotic for it. With \(u = F_{\sigma_{\max}}(x)\),

\[ \Psi(x) \;\approx\; \begin{cases} -1 + \sigma_{\min}\, \Phi^{-1}(2u), & u < \tfrac12,\\[2pt] +1 + \sigma_{\min}\, \Phi^{-1}(2u - 1), & u > \tfrac12. \end{cases} \]

Read as a formula, the applied transport is a soft sign: to leading order it collapses every input to \(\pm 1\) — Theorem 4’s limit, now visible term by term — while the \(O(\sigma_{\min})\) residue records where within its cluster the sample lands: the conserved quantile, miniaturized into the stair. Concretely, for \(\sigma_{\max} = 3\), \(\sigma_{\min} = 10^{-2}\):

\(x\) \(u = F_{3}(x)\) exact \(\Psi(x)\) stair asymptotic
\(0\) \(0.5000\) \(0\) (Lemma 3) \(0\)
\(1\) \(0.6238\) \(0.9932\) \(0.9932\)
\(2\) \(0.7360\) \(0.9993\) \(0.9993\)
\(6\) \(0.9712\) \(1.0158\) \(1.0158\)

Agreement to every displayed digit — and the exact column consists of the same numbers the ODE integration produces in the evidence paragraph below: one map, computed three independent ways (integrated flow, bisected quantile transport, stair asymptotics). The table also teaches how the map thinks: \(x = 1\) and \(x = 2\), though far apart, both land within \(10^{-2}\) of \(+1\) — the cluster forgets the details — while the extreme draw \(x = 6\) lands beyond the atom, at \(1.0158\), because its within-cluster quantile \(\Phi^{-1}(0.9424) \approx 1.58\) is carried, shrunk by \(\sigma_{\min}\), into the stair. The transport is \(\operatorname{sign}(x)\) plus quantile bookkeeping. And the transport problem that opened this section is now solved twice over, with the two solutions identified: \(M = Q = \Psi\).

Layer 2 — the mechanism: the flow conserves quantiles

The identity of Layer 1 is proved but not yet explained: why should an ODE driven by local score information reproduce a map built from global CDFs? The answer is a conservation law, and it takes two lines.

Work in the variance clock (\(\bar p_\tau, \bar F_\tau, \bar s_\tau\) as fixed in the conventions above). Each mixture component \(\mathcal{N}(x; \pm 1, \tau)\) is a heat kernel, so the family satisfies the heat equation \(\partial_\tau \bar p_\tau = \tfrac12 \partial_x^2 \bar p_\tau\) (differentiate a Gaussian once in \(\tau\) and twice in \(x\) to check; linearity does the rest). Integrating from \(-\infty\) to \(x\), using \(\partial_x \bar p_\tau \to 0\) at \(-\infty\), gives the CDF’s equation of motion:

\[ \partial_\tau \bar F_\tau(x) = \tfrac12\, \partial_x \bar p_\tau(x). \]

Now let \(x(\tau)\) be any trajectory of the probability-flow ODE, \(\dot x = v(x, \tau) = -\tfrac12\, \bar s_\tau(x)\) (the reverse sampler traverses the same curves toward smaller \(\tau\) — reversed limits, same field). By the chain rule,

\[ \frac{d}{d\tau} \bar F_\tau\big(x(\tau)\big) = \partial_\tau \bar F_\tau(x) + \bar p_\tau(x)\, \dot x = \tfrac12\, \partial_x \bar p_\tau(x) - \bar p_\tau(x) \cdot \tfrac12\, \partial_x \log \bar p_\tau(x) = 0 . \]

The quantile \(u = \bar F_\tau(x_\tau)\) is a conserved quantity of the flow. Three things follow at once.

  • Layer 1, re-derived in one line. A trajectory started at \(x_T\) carries \(u = F_{\sigma_{\max}}(x_T)\) unchanged, so it must terminate at \(F_{\sigma_{\min}}^{-1}(u)\) — Corollary 8 again, obtained now without the rigidity argument.
  • No-crossing, explained. Distinct trajectories carry distinct conserved quantiles, and \(\bar F_\tau\) is strictly increasing — so they can never meet. Proposition 6’s order-preservation is the shadow of a conservation law.
Trajectories are iso-quantile curves

For each fixed \(u \in (0,1)\), the level set \(\bar F_\tau(x) = u\) traces a smooth curve through the \((x, \tau)\) plane,

\[ \tau \;\longmapsto\; x_u(\tau) = \bar F_\tau^{-1}(u) \]

— the iso-quantile curve of the evolving family: the locus of “the \(u\)-th quantile” as the noise level changes. The conservation law says exactly this: the flow’s trajectories are these curves. Every particle rides its own quantile line and never leaves it. And every map in this tutorial is a reading of this one bundle of curves:

  • the per-leg quantile transport \(Q_k\) reads one curve at two adjacent ladder levels;
  • the end-to-end flow map \(\Psi\) reads it at the top and bottom levels;
  • the one-shot map \(T_{\sigma_{\min}}\) parameterizes the curves by \(u\) itself and reads the bottom;
  • the staircase limit is the geometry of the whole bundle as \(\tau \downarrow 0\): every curve with \(u < \tfrac12\) funnels into \(-1\), every curve with \(u > \tfrac12\) funnels into \(+1\), and the single \(u = \tfrac12\) curve — the separatrix — stays pinned at \(0\).

One family of curves; the one-shot map, the flow, and the sampler are three ways of reading it — at the endpoint, continuously, and at ladder resolution.

Layer 3 — the factorization, and why “numerical solution” is literal

The conservation law upgrades naturally into an algebra of maps: three exact moves, followed by the Euler analysis.

Move 1 — at every step of the ladder, a quantile transport is available. Proposition 2 was stated at one fixed \(\sigma\), but its proof used nothing special about the level: every \(F_s\) with \(s > 0\) is a smooth, strictly increasing bijection onto \((0,1)\). Hence for each adjacent pair of the ladder \(\sigma_{\max} = \sigma_0 > \sigma_1 > \cdots > \sigma_K = \sigma_{\min}\), the per-step quantile transport

\[ Q_k \;:=\; Q_{p_{\sigma_k} \to\, p_{\sigma_{k+1}}} \;=\; F_{\sigma_{k+1}}^{-1} \circ F_{\sigma_k} \]

is well-defined, and by Proposition 7 it is the unique monotone map carrying level \(\sigma_k\) to level \(\sigma_{k+1}\). The one-shot problem has been sliced into \(K\) sub-problems of the same kind, each with its own one-shot solution.

Move 2 — each leg of the exact flow is that per-step quantile transport. Layer 1’s argument applies verbatim on the sub-interval: the flow leg \(\Psi_{\sigma_k \to \sigma_{k+1}}\) is continuous and strictly increasing (no crossing) and pushes \(p_{\sigma_k}\) to \(p_{\sigma_{k+1}}\) (the flow reproduces the family), so by uniqueness \(\Psi_{\sigma_k \to \sigma_{k+1}} = Q_k\). Equivalently, by Layer 2: each leg carries every particle along its iso-quantile curve from one level to the next — quantile in, same quantile out.

Move 3 — telescoping identifies the composition with the one-shot map. Two independent derivations give the same identity. Dynamically, solution maps of an ODE compose — solving from \(\sigma_0\) down to \(\sigma_K\) while pausing at intermediate levels changes nothing, by uniqueness of solutions:

\[ \Psi_{\sigma_0 \to \sigma_K} = \Psi_{\sigma_{K-1} \to \sigma_K} \circ \cdots \circ \Psi_{\sigma_0 \to \sigma_1} = Q_{K-1} \circ \cdots \circ Q_0 . \]

Analytically, the same chain is pure cancellation — each intermediate CDF annihilates its own inverse:

\[ \big(F_{\sigma_K}^{-1} \circ F_{\sigma_{K-1}}\big) \circ \cdots \circ \big(F_{\sigma_1}^{-1} \circ F_{\sigma_0}\big) = F_{\sigma_K}^{-1} \circ F_{\sigma_0} = Q_{p_{\sigma_{\max}} \to\, p_{\sigma_{\min}}} . \]

Together: the one-shot quantile transport factorizes exactly into per-level quantile transports. Nothing is approximate yet — the exact reverse flow is not an approximation of the one-shot map but a factorization of it.

The last move — connecting the exact factors to the practical sampler — is where “numerical solution” acquires its literal meaning, so it deserves a derivation rather than a remark.

Notation first. Re-index the ladder by the variance clock: \(\tau := \sigma^2\) and \(\tau_k := \sigma_k^2\), with \(\Delta_k := \tau_k - \tau_{k+1} > 0\) the length of the \(k\)-th leg. Then \(\Psi_{\tau_k \to \tau_{k+1}}\) denotes the same exact flow leg as \(\Psi_{\sigma_k \to \sigma_{k+1}}\) — one object, two indexings — and by Move 2 it equals the per-step quantile transport \(Q_k\).

The exact leg, in integral form. The probability-flow ODE is \(dx/d\tau = -\tfrac12\, \bar s_\tau(x)\); the sampler traverses it toward smaller \(\tau\), which reverses the limits of integration, not the sign of the field. Integrating one leg from \(\tau_k\) down to \(\tau_{k+1}\), started at \(x\):

\[ \Psi_{\tau_k \to \tau_{k+1}}(x) = x + \int_{\tau_{k+1}}^{\tau_k} \tfrac12\, \bar s_\tau\big(x(\tau)\big)\, d\tau , \]

where \(x(\tau)\) is the trajectory itself. This is exact — but not directly evaluable as written, because the integrand must be evaluated along the very trajectory being solved for.

The one approximation. Over a short leg, freeze the integrand at the leg’s known starting data \((x, \tau_k)\):

\[ \int_{\tau_{k+1}}^{\tau_k} \tfrac12\, \bar s_\tau\big(x(\tau)\big)\, d\tau = \frac{\Delta_k}{2}\, \bar s_{\tau_k}(x) \;+\; R_k(x). \]

The frozen version defines the step map

\[ E_k(x) \;:=\; x + \frac{\Delta_k}{2}\, \bar s_{\tau_k}(x) \]

— symbol for symbol, the reverse-diffusion sampler update. Freezing the velocity at the left endpoint of each step is the definition of the explicit Euler method, so the sampler is the Euler discretization of the reverse ODE by construction, not by analogy. The remainder is second order: fix a compact set \(C\) of starting points and a compact tube containing all exact trajectories from \(C\) (such a tube exists because the field’s spatial derivative is globally bounded on the band, as in Proposition 6), and let \(L\) bound the field’s derivatives on that tube. Along the leg the integrand then drifts by \(O(\Delta_k)\), and it is integrated over an interval of length \(\Delta_k\), so, uniformly on the tube,

\[ \big| E_k(x) - \Psi_{\tau_k \to \tau_{k+1}}(x) \big| = |R_k(x)| \;\le\; \tfrac{L}{2}\, \Delta_k^2 . \]

Read through Move 2, this inequality is the statement that each step of reverse diffusion is a per-level quantile transport, correct to first order: \(E_k = Q_k + O(\Delta_k^2)\).

From one step to the whole ladder. The sampler is the composition \(S_K := E_{K-1} \circ \cdots \circ E_0\); the exact transport is \(\Psi = Q_{K-1} \circ \cdots \circ Q_0\) (Move 3). Compare them by swapping one factor at a time. Write \(z_k := (Q_{k-1} \circ \cdots \circ Q_0)(x)\) for the exact state entering leg \(k\), and \(A_k := E_{K-1} \circ \cdots \circ E_{k+1}\) for the remaining Euler tail. Inserting the hybrid compositions and telescoping gives the pointwise identity

\[ S_K(x) - \Psi(x) = \sum_{k=0}^{K-1} \Big[ A_k\big(E_k(z_k)\big) - A_k\big(Q_k(z_k)\big) \Big] \]

(nonlinear maps do not distribute over subtraction, so the difference must remain inside the tail \(A_k\)). Each \(E_j\) has derivative \(1 + \tfrac{\Delta_j}{2}\, \partial_x \bar s_{\tau_j}\), hence Lipschitz constant at most \(1 + L\Delta_j \le e^{L \Delta_j}\); every tail \(A_k\) is therefore Lipschitz with constant at most \(\Lambda := e^{L(\tau_{\max} - \tau_{\min})}\), independently of \(K\). Applying this to each bracket and then the local error bound \(|E_k(z_k) - Q_k(z_k)| \le \tfrac{L}{2}\Delta_k^2\),

\[ |S_K(x) - \Psi(x)| \;\le\; \Lambda \sum_{k=0}^{K-1} \tfrac{L}{2}\, \Delta_k^2 \;\le\; \tfrac{\Lambda L}{2}\, (\tau_{\max} - \tau_{\min})\, \max_k \Delta_k \]

which is \(O(1/K)\) for any quasi-uniform ladder (\(\max_k \Delta_k = O(1/K)\) in the variance clock). (The constants are finite for every \(\sigma_{\min} > 0\), though they grow as \(\sigma_{\min} \downarrow 0\) — the stiffness recorded under Scope.) Letting the mesh \(\max_k \Delta_k \to 0\):

\[ \boxed{\; \lim_{\max_k \Delta_k \to 0}\; E_{K-1} \circ \cdots \circ E_0 \;=\; \Psi \;=\; Q_{\,p_{\sigma_{\max}} \to\, p_{\sigma_{\min}}} \;} \]

uniformly on compact sets. The sampler and the one-shot map are not merely similar: the sampler converges to the one-shot map, at the Euler rate, and the derivation identifies every intermediate object along the way:

\[ \underbrace{F_{\sigma_{\min}}^{-1} \circ F_{\sigma_{\max}}}_{\text{one-shot solution}} \;=\; \underbrace{Q_{K-1} \circ \cdots \circ Q_0}_{\text{exact factorization (Move 3)}} \;\approx\; \underbrace{E_{K-1} \circ \cdots \circ E_0}_{\substack{\text{reverse diffusion: the sampler } S_K\\ \text{Euler, error } O(1/K)}} \]

Reverse diffusion is a numerical solution of the one-shot transport problem in the same sense that Euler’s method is a numerical solution of an initial-value problem — because it is Euler’s method, applied to an ODE whose exact solution map is the one-shot transport. Not an analogy: an instance, now derived.

The evidence — testing all three clauses. Setup, fully specified: the ladder is geometric in \(\sigma\) (uniform in \(\log \sigma\)) with \(K = 20{,}000\) legs from \(\sigma_{\max} = 3\) to \(\sigma_{\min} = 10^{-2}\); the update is the derived \(E_k\); the starting grid is \(33\) equispaced points on \([-8, 8]\) with the near-separatrix points \(|x| < 0.25\) excluded (at \(x = 0\) both maps give \(0\) exactly, by symmetry; the numerical difficulty concentrates near the separatrix, where the stiffness peaks); errors are suprema over the grid. Results: \(\sup |S_K - Q| \approx 1.9 \times 10^{-5}\) — clause (i) and the Euler side of clause (iii), whose \(O(1/K)\) signature Exercise 8 has you measure. And clause (ii) tested directly: the conserved quantile \(\bar F_{\tau_k}(x_k)\), monitored along every trajectory, drifts from its initial value by at most \(9.8 \times 10^{-5}\) over all twenty thousand steps — an Euler-sized violation of an exact conservation law, exactly as the theory prescribes.

Why the incremental route is the one that scales. The factorization is also the interface between the mathematics and the learning. The one-shot map is globally wild — in the \(\sigma \to 0\) limit, discontinuous — and is built from the CDF, a global object unavailable in high dimension. Each Euler factor \(E_k\), by contrast, is near-identity and consumes only the score at a single noise level — a local quantity that is exactly a regression target (denoising); the exact factor \(Q_k\) would still need the two endpoint CDFs, so it is the Euler step, not the exact leg, that is purely local. A composition of many learnable maps realizes one unlearnable map. Structurally, this is a homotopy (continuation) method for sampling: embed the intractable target in a family \(\{p_\sigma\}\) terminating at an easy, approximately Gaussian high-noise law, and track the solution path — equivalently, by Layer 2, track each sample’s conserved quantile — one level at a time.

Scope. Two caveats frame the result. First, the identification of the flow with the quantile transport is special to one dimension, where “monotone” pins the transport down uniquely; in higher dimensions the probability-flow ODE defines a valid transport that is in general not the monotone (optimal-transport) map. Second, the statement concerns the exact score, exact terminal law, and exact integration; and the velocity field becomes increasingly stiff as \(\sigma_{\min} \downarrow 0\) — for this example the score’s derivative at the separatrix grows like \(\sigma^{-4}\) (compute: \(s'(0) = 1/\sigma^4 - 1/\sigma^2\)) — so the flow is smooth at every positive noise level even though its limit is discontinuous, which is the dynamical face of the boundary layer of Theorem 4.

Interpretation

The same step function has now appeared on both sides of the generative pipeline. In data space, the posterior-mean denoiser — the conditional mean \(\mathbb{E}[x_0 \mid x] = \tanh(x/\sigma^2)\) of the clean point given a noisy observation, the object diffusion models learn — sharpens into \(\operatorname{sign}(x)\). In noise space, the generator \(T_\sigma(u) \to \operatorname{sign}(u - \tfrac12)\). In both cases a smooth object degenerates into a discontinuous one precisely as the noise scale vanishes. This is the one-dimensional instance of a general phenomenon: discrete or lower-dimensional data distributions are reached as limits of smooth transports, and learnable models operate at small positive noise scales exactly because smooth maps can be represented and trained while their discontinuous limits cannot. Diffusion models anneal \(\sigma\) toward zero rather than starting there for this reason.
