The World’s Smallest Diffusion Model

Standalone tutorial accompanying W01. Interactive version: live demo — every figure in this note, animated.

Diffusion models generate images from pure noise. Before we spend forty lectures on the mathematics, this note builds a complete diffusion model with a dataset of two points — small enough that every quantity the course will later approximate with a neural network can be computed exactly, with nothing beyond Bayes’ rule.

The dataset

Our data distribution puts equal mass on two points:

\[ p_{\text{data}}(x_0) \;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\,\delta(x_0 + 1) \;+\; \tfrac{1}{2}\,\delta(x_0 - 1), \qquad x_0 \in \{-1, +1\}. \]

Generative modeling asks: given samples from \(p_{\text{data}}\), produce more samples. For this dataset the answer is a coin flip — which is exactly why it is the right dataset: the machinery becomes transparent.

Forward process: noise melts the data

Corrupt a clean sample with Gaussian noise of size \(\sigma\):

\[ x_\sigma \;=\; x_0 + \sigma z, \qquad z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1) \text{ independent of } x_0. \]

Since \(x_0 = \pm 1\) with probability \(\tfrac12\) each, the density of the corrupted sample is a two-component Gaussian mixture:

\[ p_\sigma(x) \;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\,\mathcal{N}(x;\, -1,\, \sigma^2) \;+\; \tfrac{1}{2}\,\mathcal{N}(x;\, +1,\, \sigma^2). \]

Two facts, both visible in the demo:

  • Small \(\sigma\): two sharp bumps at \(\pm 1\) — the data is intact.
  • Large \(\sigma\): the bumps merge. Indeed \(\mathbb{E}[x_\sigma] = 0\) and \(\operatorname{Var}(x_\sigma) = \mathbb{E}[x_0^2] + \sigma^2 = 1 + \sigma^2\), and for \(\sigma \gtrsim 3\) the mixture is indistinguishable in practice from \(\mathcal{N}(0,\, 1 + \sigma^2)\) — at \(\sigma = 3\) its excess kurtosis is only \(-0.02\). (It is never exactly Gaussian at finite \(\sigma\); the precise statement is \(x_\sigma / \sqrt{1+\sigma^2} \Rightarrow \mathcal{N}(0,1)\) as \(\sigma \to \infty\).)

This is the first design principle of diffusion models: destroying structure is easy, fully understood, and ends somewhere known (a Gaussian), no matter what the data was.

Denoising: Bayes’ rule gives the optimal denoiser in closed form

Now invert: we observe a noisy \(x_\sigma = x\) and want the clean \(x_0\). The best guess under squared error is the posterior mean \(\mathbb{E}[x_0 \mid x_\sigma = x]\) (we will prove this optimality claim in Module 1; for now, take it).

The posterior over the two candidates follows from Bayes’ rule:

\[ \mathbb{P}(x_0 = +1 \mid x) = \frac{\tfrac12\,\mathcal{N}(x; +1, \sigma^2)} {\tfrac12\,\mathcal{N}(x; +1, \sigma^2) + \tfrac12\,\mathcal{N}(x; -1, \sigma^2)} = \frac{1}{1 + \exp\!\big(-\tfrac{2x}{\sigma^2}\big)} = \operatorname{sigmoid}\!\Big(\frac{2x}{\sigma^2}\Big), \]

where the middle step divides through by \(\mathcal{N}(x; +1, \sigma^2)\) and uses \(\exp\!\big(-\tfrac{(x+1)^2}{2\sigma^2}\big) \big/ \exp\!\big(-\tfrac{(x-1)^2}{2\sigma^2}\big) = \exp\!\big(-\tfrac{2x}{\sigma^2}\big)\). Therefore

\[ \boxed{\;\mathbb{E}[x_0 \mid x_\sigma = x] = (+1)\,\mathbb{P}(+1 \mid x) + (-1)\,\mathbb{P}(-1 \mid x) = \tanh\!\Big(\frac{x}{\sigma^2}\Big).\;} \]

The optimal denoiser is a tanh — an activation function you already know, produced by nothing but probability. Sanity-check its two regimes:

  • \(\sigma \to 0\): \(\tanh(x/\sigma^2)\) becomes a hard step — snap to the nearer data point.
  • \(\sigma \to \infty\): \(\tanh(x/\sigma^2) \approx x/\sigma^2 \to 0\) — the observation is all noise, so the best guess is the prior mean \(0\).

The score function (a preview of Module 4)

Differentiate \(\log p_\sigma\) directly. With \(\varphi_\pm(x) = \mathcal{N}(x; \pm 1, \sigma^2)\) and \(\varphi_\pm'(x) = -\tfrac{x \mp 1}{\sigma^2}\varphi_\pm(x)\),

\[ \nabla_x \log p_\sigma(x) = \frac{\tfrac12\varphi_-'(x) + \tfrac12\varphi_+'(x)}{p_\sigma(x)} = -\frac{x - \mathbb{E}[x_0 \mid x]}{\sigma^2} = \frac{\tanh\!\big(\tfrac{x}{\sigma^2}\big) - x}{\sigma^2}. \]

Rearranged, this is Tweedie’s formula, one of the two or three most important identities of the course:

\[ \mathbb{E}[x_0 \mid x_\sigma = x] \;=\; x + \sigma^2\, \nabla_x \log p_\sigma(x). \]

Denoising and knowing the score are the same thing — for additive Gaussian noise of known size \(\sigma\), which is exactly the diffusion setting. The score \(\nabla_x \log p_\sigma\) points from \(x\) toward where the (noised) data is denser; the denoiser just moves by \(\sigma^2\) times the score. One caveat: the score is a local quantity — at \(x = 0\) it vanishes by symmetry even though, in the two-bump regime, \(x = 0\) is a density minimum. It is an unstable equilibrium: the reverse dynamics leave a particle sitting exactly there alone, and push a particle on either side away toward the nearer mode.

Reverse process: run the diffusion backwards

Generation now works by annealing: start from pure noise at a large \(\sigma_{\max}\), and lower \(\sigma\) along a grid \(\sigma_{\max} = \sigma_0 > \sigma_1 > \cdots > \sigma_K \approx 0\), nudging the sample along the score at every step. (We write \(y_k\) for the reverse iterate at noise level \(\sigma_k\), keeping \(x_0\) reserved for the clean data point; \(y_0\) is pure noise and \(y_K\) is the generated sample.)

\[ y_{k+1} \;=\; y_k \;+\; \frac{\sigma_k^2 - \sigma_{k+1}^2}{2}\, \nabla_y \log p_{\sigma_k}(y_k) \;=\; y_k + \frac{\sigma_k^2 - \sigma_{k+1}^2}{2}\cdot \frac{\tanh\!\big(y_k/\sigma_k^2\big) - y_k}{\sigma_k^2}. \]

This deterministic rule is the explicit Euler discretization of the probability-flow ODE in the variance clock \(\tau = \sigma^2\). (Strictly, to speak of dynamics across noise levels we couple them: \(x_\tau = x_0 + w_\tau\) with \(w\) a Brownian motion — Module 5 makes this precise; nothing below needs it yet.) The stochastic variant both doubles the score drift and injects fresh independent noise at each step,

\[ y_{k+1} = y_k + \big(\sigma_k^2 - \sigma_{k+1}^2\big)\,\nabla_y \log p_{\sigma_k}(y_k) + \sqrt{\sigma_k^2 - \sigma_{k+1}^2}\; z_k, \qquad z_k \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1) \text{ i.i.d.}, \]

giving the Euler–Maruyama discretization of the reverse-time SDE. Where these updates come from is the business of Modules 5–6 (Fokker–Planck, reverse-time dynamics); today we only need to watch them work: a cloud of particles started from \(\mathcal{N}(0,\, 1+\sigma_{\max}^2)\) splits, condenses, and lands within \(\approx \sigma_{\min}\) of \(\{-1, +1\}\), in equal proportions up to sampling error.

To be honest about the word “exact”: the formulas are exact, but the sampler carries four small approximations — we initialize from the moment-matched Gaussian rather than the true mixture \(p_{\sigma_{\max}}\) (indistinguishable at \(\sigma_{\max} = 3\), but not equal); we stop at \(\sigma_{\min} = 10^{-2} > 0\), so samples end a hair’s width from the atoms; the Euler steps discretize the flow; and any finite run has sampling noise. Each vanishes in the appropriate limit — and every one of them has a counterpart in real diffusion models.

The full sampler, with no neural network anywhere:

import numpy as np

def score(y, s2):                 # ∇ log p_σ(y), exact for the two-point data
    return (np.tanh(y / s2) - y) / s2

smax, smin, K, N = 3.0, 1e-2, 500, 100_000
sig = smax * (smin / smax) ** (np.arange(K + 1) / K)    # geometric σ grid
y = np.sqrt(1 + smax**2) * np.random.randn(N)           # start from pure noise

for k in range(K):                                       # probability-flow ODE
    d = sig[k]**2 - sig[k + 1]**2
    y += 0.5 * d * score(y, sig[k]**2)

err = np.abs(np.abs(y) - 1).max()      # worst distance to the nearest data point
print(err, (y > 0).mean())   # ≈ 0 and ≈ 0.5, up to σ_min + discretization + sampling

Why the course exists: what breaks for images

Everything above was exact because the posterior mean had a formula. For images there is no usable one. (Exercise 4 gives a closed form for any finite training set — but a sampler built on it can only ever reproduce the training points; what we actually want is the posterior mean under the unknown population distribution.) Nor can it be tabulated: a \(64 \times 64\) RGB image lives in a space of \(256^{12288}\) configurations, and the data occupies a vanishingly thin filament of it (Module 1: the curse of dimensionality). The entire role of the neural network in a diffusion model is to approximate the map \((x, \sigma) \mapsto \mathbb{E}[x_0 \mid x]\) — to be the tanh we can no longer derive. Every other ingredient of this note survives unchanged, and each becomes a module of the course:

Exercises

  1. Fill in the algebra for \(\mathbb{P}(x_0 = +1 \mid x) = \operatorname{sigmoid}(2x/\sigma^2)\) and \(\mathbb{E}[x_0 \mid x] = \tanh(x/\sigma^2)\).
  2. Show \(\operatorname{Var}(x_\sigma) = 1 + \sigma^2\), and more generally \(\operatorname{Var}(x_\sigma) = \operatorname{Var}(x_0) + \sigma^2\) for any data distribution with finite variance (independence of \(z\) and \(x_0\) is all you need — zero mean is not required).
  3. Unequal weights. Let \(\mathbb{P}(x_0 = +1) = \pi\) with \(0 < \pi < 1\). (Convention warning: this is the opposite of tutorials 01 and 02, which set \(\pi = \mathbb{P}(-1)\). The formulas differ by \(\pi \to 1 - \pi\); check which one you are reading.) Show \(\mathbb{E}[x_0 \mid x] = \tanh\!\big(\tfrac{x}{\sigma^2} + \tfrac12 \log\tfrac{\pi}{1-\pi}\big)\) — the denoiser acquires a bias. Check that as \(\sigma \to \infty\) this tends to \(2\pi - 1\), the prior mean, and work out what happens in the degenerate cases \(\pi \in \{0, 1\}\) by taking limits.
  4. More points. For data uniform on \(\{a_1, \dots, a_m\}\), show \(\mathbb{E}[x_0 \mid x] = \sum_i a_i\, w_i(x)\) with \(w_i(x) \propto \exp\!\big(\tfrac{a_i x}{\sigma^2} - \tfrac{a_i^2}{2\sigma^2}\big) \propto \exp\!\big(-\tfrac{(x - a_i)^2}{2\sigma^2}\big)\) — a softmax with Gaussian-kernel weights. (So the optimal denoiser for a finite dataset under Gaussian corruption is attention-like: an RBF-weighted average of the training points. For non-uniform weights \(\pi_i\), each logit gains \(\log \pi_i\).)
  5. Run the sampler above with the SDE update instead of the ODE. Compare the RMS distance of the final samples to the nearest data point for both, as a function of \(K\) and of \(\sigma_{\min}\). (You should find that increasing \(K\) removes discretization error but both samplers retain a terminal width \(\approx \sigma_{\min}\) — lowering \(\sigma_{\min}\), not raising \(K\), is what sharpens the atoms.)
