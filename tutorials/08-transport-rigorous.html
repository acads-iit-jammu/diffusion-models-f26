Generative Models as Transport: History and Validation

The rigorous twin of Moving Distributions: The Transport Story. This tutorial states the transport view of generative modeling precisely, traces its history, and validates — or corrects — each of its claims against theorems and the literature. Every load-bearing citation below was checked against its source; the two adversarial review passes that shaped this document flagged, in particular, two category errors that the folklore version of the thesis commits, and both are repaired here.

The thesis, and two category errors to avoid

The folklore version of the transport view says: generative modeling is fundamentally transport; in 1-D the inverse CDF solves it completely and all model families coincide there; in higher dimension the transport is no longer unique, and VAEs, GANs, flows, and diffusion models are different general constructions. This is close to right — and wrong in two instructive ways.

Category error 1: representation is not explanation. On standard Borel spaces, essentially every distribution is the pushforward of a uniform random variable by some measurable map; a stochastic sampler becomes a deterministic one after adjoining its auxiliary noise to the source. “Every generative model is a transport” is therefore nearly tautological — a representation available for anything, not a characterization of anything. What the transport language genuinely does not capture: tractability of the likelihood (flows buy it with Jacobians, the probability-flow ODE with divergence integration, VAEs with an evidence bound, GANs not at all); conditioning (a coherent family \(p(dx \mid c)\) is more structure than an endpoint map); stochastic kernels (a VAE decoder is a Markov kernel, not a map, and its encoder approximates a posterior kernel, not an inverse); and — decisively for what follows — coupling identification: two maps can have identical pushforwards and completely different input–output correspondences. Matching the endpoint law does not identify the map.

Category error 2: 1-D uniqueness is a property of a selection rule, not of dimension. Even on the real line, transport is wildly nonunique: both \(u \mapsto u\) and \(u \mapsto 1-u\) carry \(\mathrm{Uniform}(0,1)\) to itself, and composing any generator with a source-preserving map changes nothing about the output law. What dimension one possesses is a uniquely privileged selection: the total order, and with it the requirement “order-preserving,” which singles out one map. Higher dimensions do not lose transport; they lose the privileged tie-breaker.

With both corrections made, the thesis survives in a sharper form, stated at the end of this tutorial. The body earns it.

Dimension one: the complete theory of order-compatible transport

Fix probability measures \(\mu, \nu\) on \(\mathbb{R}\) with CDFs \(F_\mu, F_\nu\) and quantile functions \(Q_\mu(u) = \inf\{x : F_\mu(x) \ge u\}\) (generalized inverses, defined for all laws, atoms included).

Fact 1 (generation always works — atoms included). \(Q_{\nu\#}\, \mathrm{Uniform}(0,1) = \nu\) for every law \(\nu\) on \(\mathbb{R}\). The quantile coupling \(\gamma^\uparrow = (Q_\mu, Q_\nu)_\#\, \mathrm{Leb}_{(0,1)}\) exists for arbitrary \(\mu, \nu\) and is the canonical order-preserving (comonotone) coupling. (Standard; see Santambrogio, Optimal Transport for Applied Mathematicians, Ch. 2.)

Fact 2 (uniqueness, under the order selection, for atomless sources). If \(\mu\) is atomless, then \(T = Q_\nu \circ F_\mu\) is the unique nondecreasing map with \(T_\# \mu = \nu\), up to \(\mu\)-null sets. The target may be atomic — this course’s two-point distribution is generated by exactly such a map, and the full proof is in The Deterministic Map, Proved (Theorem 1 and Propositions 2 and 7 there).

Fact 3 (optimality, for free). The same monotone map is optimal for every convex displacement cost \(c(x - y)\) — a universality that is essentially one-dimensional and will fail upstairs.

Fact 4 (the honest direction of the atomic caveat). The failure mode is not generation into atomic targets — that always works — but deterministic encoding out of atomic sources: a source atom cannot be split by a map, so no deterministic encoder carries atomic data onto a continuous prior. The canonical repair is stochastic — Rüschendorf’s distributional transform \(U = F_\mu(X^-) + V\,(F_\mu(X) - F_\mu(X^-))\) with \(V \sim \mathrm{Uniform}(0,1)\) independent, which is exactly uniform even when \(X\) has atoms. (Randomized rank-breaking of ties: the coin flip of the informal tutorial.)

So the precise sense in which dimension one is “completely understood”: order-compatible transport on the line has an explicit, generalized-quantile solution in all cases. Not solved by this: transport under other selections, statistical estimation, or learnability — distinctions that matter below.

Dimension two and above: underdeterminacy, and the selection principles

Endpoint marginals never determine a coupling: if \(T_\# \rho = \mu\) and \(S_\# \rho = \rho\), then \((T \circ S)_\# \rho = \mu\). In \(d \ge 2\) the supply of source-preserving maps \(S\) is enormous, and — the genuine loss — no total order exists to privilege one choice. There is a second, subtler underdeterminacy at the level of dynamics: for the continuity equation \(\partial_t p + \nabla\!\cdot(p v) = 0\), if \(v\) transports the path \(\{p_t\}\) then so does \(v + w\) for any \(w\) with \(\nabla\!\cdot(p w) = 0\) — weighted divergence-free “gauge” fields, abundant in \(d \ge 2\), invisible in \(d = 1\) (no room to swirl on a line). Even a prescribed marginal path does not determine particle trajectories; a current must be chosen too.

Each classical and each modern construction is a selection principle — something added to the marginal-matching requirement to pin down a map, a coupling, or a path. The audit table (each row’s qualification is part of the claim):

Construction What selects Essential qualification
Brenier / optimal transport minimize squared displacement unique map \(T = \nabla \phi\), \(\phi\) convex, for absolutely continuous sources with second moments — canonical relative to Euclidean quadratic geometry (Brenier 1991; McCann 1995)
Knothe–Rosenblatt sequential conditional quantiles unique monotone triangular map after fixing a coordinate order; not rotation-invariant, generally not optimal (Rosenblatt 1952; Knothe 1957; Carlier–Galichon–Santambrogio 2009)
Autoregressive flows triangular architecture + likelihood an expressive monotone triangular flow can recover KR; permuted stacks need not equal any single KR map (Irons et al. 2022)
General normalizing flows architecture, orientation, optimization maximum likelihood identifies the density, not the map
Diffusion / probability-flow ODE forward SDE + schedule + score + choice of current the path \(\{p_t\}\) alone does not fix the velocity in \(d \ge 2\); the gradient-form (curl-free relative to \(p\)) current is part of the selection
Flow matching / rectified flow endpoint coupling + interpolant + regression different couplings/interpolants give different fields with identical endpoints (Lipman et al. 2023; Liu et al. 2023)
GAN none any transport fooling the discriminator is accepted; even WGAN’s optimal-transport problem lives between the real and generated laws in data space — it does not select a latent-to-data map (Goodfellow et al. 2014; Arjovsky et al. 2017)
VAE ELBO + prior + decoder noise + architecture selects a generally nonidentifiable joint model and posterior kernel, not a Monge map
Schrödinger bridge path-space relative entropy to a reference process the principled stochastic transport selection (Léonard 2014)

And the statistics community, needing multivariate quantiles for its own reasons, ran the same route: the center-outward distribution functions of Chernozhukov–Galichon–Hallin–Henry (2017) and Hallin et al. (2021, arXiv:1806.01238) define the multivariate CDF as the Brenier map to a reference spherical law — canonical relative to that chosen reference geometry, not choice-free. The lesson of the whole table in one line: beyond dimension one, “the transport” is a category mistake; only “the transport selected by ⟨rule⟩” is well-posed.

The 1-D coincidences, stratified by epistemic status

The folklore says “all families coincide with the quantile map in 1-D.” The truth splits three ways, and the split is the pedagogy.

Theorem-level (all exact scalar ODE samplers — one proof covers them). Let \(\{p_t\}\) be a smooth positive scalar density path with velocity \(v\) solving the continuity equation with zero flux at \(-\infty\). Then \(\partial_t F_t = -p_t v_t\), and along any characteristic \(\dot X = v_t(X)\): \(\frac{d}{dt} F_t(X_t) = 0\) — quantile labels are conserved, so the endpoint map is the quantile transport. This single argument covers the diffusion probability-flow ODE (proved in full, with numerics, in The Deterministic Map, Proved), continuous normalizing flows, flow matching, and rectified flow at the exact/population level; the distinctions among them are about how \(p_t\) and \(v_t\) are chosen and estimated, not about the exact 1-D endpoint. Monotone discrete-time flows join by Fact 2 directly. (Practical training adds estimation and integration error — conditionality of practice, not of mathematics.)

Available-but-not-selected (GANs). An explicit counterexample: with \(U \sim \mathrm{Uniform}(0,1)\), the tent map \(s(u) = 2u \wedge (2 - 2u)\) preserves uniformity, so \(Q_\mu(s(U))\) generates \(\mu\) exactly — and is not the quantile map. No idealized adversarial objective can distinguish it from \(Q_\mu(U)\): marginal matching leaves the generator unidentified. The coincidence is available to a GAN and never implied by its training.

Available-only-in-a-singular-idealization (VAEs). The precise proposition: if data and latent are scalar and atomless, the prior has continuous strictly increasing CDF \(H\), the encoder \(E\) and decoder \(D\) are deterministic, mutually inverse a.e., aggregate-matched ($E_# = $ prior), nondecreasing, and exactly optimized, then Fact 2 forces \(E = H^{-1} \circ F_\mu\) and \(D = Q_\mu \circ H\) — with a uniform prior, literally \(E = F_\mu\), \(D = Q_\mu\). Every hypothesis earns its place: drop monotonicity and the decreasing solution (and worse) appears; and a strict VAE cannot even enter this idealization — a deterministic encoder against a continuous prior has infinite KL penalty (\(\mathrm{KL}(\mathcal{N}(E(x), \sigma^2) \,\|\, \mathcal{N}(0,1)) \to \infty\) as \(\sigma \downarrow 0\)), and an expressive decoder admits posterior collapse (\(p_\theta(x \mid z) = p_{\text{data}}\), encoder carrying no information) which fits the marginal perfectly with no CDF anywhere. Identifiability theory does not rescue the slogan: the identifiable-VAE results of Khemakhem et al. (2020) require auxiliary observed variables and structured priors, deliver identification only up to linear/permutation ambiguities, and do not apply to the ordinary unconditional VAE — consistent with the classical nonidentifiability of nonlinear ICA (Hyvärinen–Pajunen 1999) and the impossibility results for unsupervised disentanglement (Locatello et al. 2019). The idealized object is better named a monotone scalar normalizing flow (or a Wasserstein-autoencoder-style construction, whose objective — Tolstikhin et al. 2018 — does regularize the aggregate encoding, unlike the VAE’s). The slogan “the encoder is the CDF” is a theorem about that idealized object and a false statement about VAE training.

The three-way split generalizes into the distinction this tutorial exists to teach:

For any generative method, ask three separate questions. (1) Existence: can it represent a transport achieving the target law? (2) Selection: does its construction single out which transport? (3) Identification: does its training objective actually enforce that selection? In 1-D, exact scalar ODE methods answer yes–yes–yes (order is built into non-crossing); GANs answer yes–no–no; VAEs answer yes–no–no with an extra obstruction (kernels, not maps). The families feel identical in 1-D only if one asks question (1) alone.

What breaks upstairs: the misleading-intuitions inventory

Each entry is a 1-D truth that fails in \(d \ge 2\) — with the failure’s certificate.

  1. “Transport is unique.” Never was — uniqueness belonged to the order selection, which does not exist upstairs.
  2. “Non-crossing implies the canonical map.” In \(d \ge 2\) trajectories can rotate and swirl without intersecting; orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms need not be (cyclically) monotone or optimal.
  3. “A gradient velocity field integrates to a gradient map.” False: time-ordered products of symmetric Jacobians lose symmetry when Hessians at different times fail to commute. This is precisely the mechanism of Lavenant–Santambrogio (2022) (Appl. Math. Lett.): a smooth two-dimensional example where the Fokker–Planck flow map is a perfectly valid transport to the Gaussian and not the Brenier map — written to refute a published conjecture (Khrulkov–Oseledets 2022) that assumed otherwise. The 1-D coincidence misled professionals in print.
  4. “The marginal path determines the particle paths.” Only on the line, where zero-flux kills the ambiguity. Upstairs, divergence-free currents are a genuine gauge freedom; the diffusion velocity is a choice of current.
  5. “Componentwise CDFs are a multivariate rank.” They uniformize marginals and leave the entire dependence structure (the copula) untouched; Rosenblatt’s transform fixes this at the price of coordinate-order dependence.
  6. “Monotone becomes meaningless.” It becomes plural: coordinatewise isotonicity, monotone operators, triangular monotonicity, and cyclic monotonicity are inequivalent notions — and different selection principles commit to different ones (Brenier: cyclic; KR: triangular).
  7. “One map is optimal for all convex costs.” Essentially one-dimensional. Upstairs the selected map depends on the cost and the geometry.
  8. “Low-dimensional pictures scale.” \(\|Z\| \approx \sqrt{d}\) for \(Z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I_d)\) — mass lives on shells, not at the mode; and empirical transport estimation suffers dimension-dependent rates of order \(n^{-1/d}\) (Weed–Bach 2019). The 1-D laboratory is a laboratory, not a scale model.

A short history, as the pedagogy’s spine

Year Event
1781 Monge poses the transport problem (déblais et remblais)
1942 Kantorovich relaxes maps to couplings — transport becomes tractable
1952 / 1957 Rosenblatt’s transformation; Knothe’s rearrangement — the coordinate-order selection
1991 / 1995 Brenier’s polar factorization; McCann’s refinements — the cost selection, uniqueness restored in \(d \ge 2\)
2013–2014 VAEs (Kingma–Welling); GANs (Goodfellow et al.) — learned transports with variational and adversarial objectives, no map selection
2015–2017 Normalizing flows mature (Rezende–Mohamed; autoregressive = triangular ≈ KR); WGAN imports the OT metric (not the OT map)
2015–2021 Diffusion: nonequilibrium thermodynamics (Sohl-Dickstein et al.), DDPM (Ho et al.), score SDE and probability-flow ODE (Song et al.) — the dynamic selection
2017–2021 Statistics rebuilds the CDF via transport: Monge–Kantorovich depth, center-outward distribution functions (Chernozhukov et al.; Hallin et al.)
2022 Khrulkov–Oseledets conjecture the diffusion flow is OT; Lavenant–Santambrogio refute it — the 1-D coincidence formally dies in \(d \ge 2\)
2022–2023 Flow matching (Lipman et al.), rectified flow (Liu et al.), stochastic interpolants (Albergo–Boffi–Vanden-Eijnden) — the coupling-and-interpolant selection made explicit

The pedagogy of this course tracks that history in miniature: quantiles first, then the loss of order, then the selections — and the stochastic-interpolant viewpoint, in which coupling, interpolant, velocity, and noise are all explicit choices, is where the modern account closes the loop.

The refined thesis

What we actually teach

Many generative samplers can be represented as maps or stochastic kernels carrying a reference law to the model law — a useful common language, not a complete characterization of generative modeling. On the real line, the total order selects a canonical quantile coupling; for atomless sources it is induced by the unique nondecreasing map, which is simultaneously optimal for every convex displacement cost. Other transports exist even there. In higher dimensions, endpoint marginals underdetermine both the coupling and the dynamics, so additional structure — a cost and its geometry (Brenier), a coordinate order (Knothe–Rosenblatt, autoregressive flows), a forward corruption process together with a choice of probability current (diffusion), an endpoint coupling and interpolant (flow matching, rectified flow, stochastic interpolants), path-space entropy (Schrödinger bridges), or merely an architecture and an objective (GANs, VAEs) — selects the construction. Exact scalar ODE samplers necessarily implement the quantile map, because non-crossing on a line is the order selection; adversarial and variational objectives do not select it, and stochastic decoders and reverse SDEs are kernels, not inverse maps. Dimension one is therefore a laboratory for separating four questions — endpoint matching, transport selection, dynamics, and learnability — not a setting in which the model families are intrinsically identical.
