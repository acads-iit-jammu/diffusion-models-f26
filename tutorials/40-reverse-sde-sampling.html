Reverse-SDE sampling

NoteDraft — Stochastic generation from a learned score

This is the next topic after Denoising Score Matching and Langevin sampling. The goal is to transport a terminal distribution back to data using a stochastic process. Data are at \(t=0\) and the terminal prior is at \(t=1\). The additional clock \(\tau=1-t\) increases during generation.

1. From a fixed target to a prescribed density path

Ordinary Langevin dynamics uses the score of one fixed target and attempts to approach its stationary distribution. Reverse diffusion instead follows a time-dependent sequence of target densities, \(p_1,p_{1-h},\ldots,p_0\). It does not wait for equilibrium at each noise level.

Choose a forward process

\[dX_t=f(X_t,t)\,dt+g(t)\,dW_t,\]

and let \(p_t\) be its marginal density. A one-step Euler–Maruyama approximation adds deterministic drift \(h f\) and independent Gaussian noise with standard deviation \(g\sqrt h\). Brownian increments scale with \(\sqrt h\), not \(h\).

Learn \(s_\theta(x,t)\approx\partial_x\log p_t(x)\) using known conditional corruption kernels. The distinction between conditional labels and marginal predictions is the same as in the preceding topic.

2. Reverse the stochastic process

Write \(Y_\tau=X_{1-\tau}\) for the time-reversed process in law. Under the usual smoothness and time-reversal conditions, and with diffusion coefficient independent of position,

\[\boxed{dY_\tau= [-f(Y_\tau,t)+g(t)^2s_t(Y_\tau)]\,d\tau +g(t)\,d\overline W_\tau,\qquad t=1-\tau.}\]

The Brownian motion is defined with respect to the reverse-time information; it is not obtained by simply negating the original Brownian increments. The formula above applies to scalar \(g(t)\); state-dependent diffusion requires additional derivative terms.

In the original decreasing clock, the same reverse dynamics is often written

\[dX_t=[f(X_t,t)-g(t)^2s_t(X_t)]\,dt+g(t)\,d\overline W_t, \qquad dt<0.\]

Noise increments in this notation have variance proportional to \(|dt|\). We use the increasing reverse clock to avoid negative-variance interpretations. The primary reference is Song et al., Eq. (6); Yang Song’s blog explains the forward and reverse SDEs as separate processes.

3. VP diffusion and an explicit sampler

For the VP forward process,

\[f(x,t)=-\tfrac12\beta(t)x,\qquad g(t)=\sqrt{\beta(t)},\]

with the same schedule relation as before:

\[\alpha_t=\exp\left[-\tfrac12\int_0^t\beta(u)\,du\right], \qquad X_t\mid X_0=x_0\sim\mathcal N(\alpha_tx_0,1-\alpha_t^2).\]

The reverse SDE is

\[dY_\tau=\beta(t)[\tfrac12Y_\tau+s_t(Y_\tau)]\,d\tau +\sqrt{\beta(t)}\,d\overline W_\tau.\]

For \(h=t_k-t_{k+1}>0\), generate with

\[\boxed{x_{k+1}=x_k+h\beta(t_k)[\tfrac12x_k+s_\theta(x_k,t_k)] +\sqrt{h\beta(t_k)}\,z_k,\qquad z_k\sim\mathcal N(0,1).}\]

Start from the actual \(p_1\) if available, or a justified approximation such as standard normal after sufficient corruption. Draw fresh independent noise at each step. Stop at a positive cutoff compatible with the trained score if the endpoint is singular. The exact continuous process has the required marginals with the exact score and matched initialization; a learned score, finite steps, and approximate prior introduce errors.

4. Why this is not an ODE with noise simply added

In increasing reverse time, the probability-flow ODE uses

\[b_{\mathrm{ODE}}=-f+\tfrac12g^2s_t,\]

while the reverse SDE uses

\[b_{\mathrm{SDE}}=-f+g^2s_t.\]

Therefore

\[b_{\mathrm{SDE}}=b_{\mathrm{ODE}}+\tfrac12g^2s_t.\]

The extra score drift compensates for the Brownian spreading. Adding \(g\,dW\) to the probability-flow ODE without that drift correction changes the density evolution. Appendix A checks the factors explicitly.

5. A Gaussian example checks the sign and coefficient

If the clean law is standard normal, VP preserves it and \(s_t(x)=-x\). The reverse SDE becomes

\[dY_\tau=-\tfrac12\beta(t)Y_\tau\,d\tau +\sqrt{\beta(t)}\,d\overline W_\tau.\]

At mean zero and variance one, the instantaneous mean and variance derivatives are zero. The density stays standard normal although particles move randomly. The corresponding probability-flow ODE has zero velocity, so its particles remain fixed. The two processes agree on marginals, not trajectories.

For constant \(\beta\) and Euler step \(h\), the stochastic update has stationary variance \(1/(1-h\beta/4)\) when \(0<h\beta<4\). Its deviation from one is a discretization effect, not a failure of the continuous reverse-SDE formula.

Appendix A. Check the reversed density evolution

The forward Fokker–Planck equation is

\[\partial_tp_t=-\partial_x(fp_t)+\tfrac12g^2\partial_{xx}p_t.\]

Set \(r_\tau=p_{1-\tau}\). Then

\[\partial_\tau r_\tau=\partial_x(fr_\tau)-\tfrac12g^2\partial_{xx}r_\tau,\]

where coefficients and scores are evaluated at \(t=1-\tau\). The proposed reverse SDE gives

\[\begin{aligned} -\partial_x[(-f+g^2s_t)r_\tau]+\tfrac12g^2\partial_{xx}r_\tau &=\partial_x(fr_\tau)-g^2\partial_x(r_\tau s_t) +\tfrac12g^2\partial_{xx}r_\tau\\ &=\partial_x(fr_\tau)-\tfrac12g^2\partial_{xx}r_\tau, \end{aligned}\]

because \(r_\tau s_t=\partial_xr_\tau\). This verifies the marginal equation. Identifying the full reversed path law requires the time-reversal result cited above; the marginal calculation alone does not establish that joint law.

For comparison, the ODE flux with \(-f+g^2s_t/2\) gives the same marginal equation without a diffusion term. See Probability-Flow ODE, Part 09 for the connection to DDPM and DDIM.
